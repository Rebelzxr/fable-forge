# Design DNA: subject-led decisions

This reference gives judgement, not a universal recipe. Patterns seen on well-made sites are useful examples, not laws for every client.

## Find a concept that belongs to this subject

Describe the page as a place, tool, publication, service, or task the visitor recognizes. Use the subject's real materials, vocabulary, constraints, and proof. The first viewport should communicate what this particular page is for; it may be typographic, photographic, illustrative, data-led, or interactive. Choose the form that clarifies the content.

For a real business, specificity comes from verified facts, concrete examples, and useful limitations. Invented dates, locations, counts, reviews, and credentials belong only in clearly labelled fiction or synthetic demonstrations.

## Compose with intention

- Establish one focal idea. Surround it with enough quiet space and hierarchy for the visitor to read and act.
- Type carries meaning through family, weight, width, scale, line breaks, and spacing. Check the actual rendered font and headline silhouette on narrow and wide screens. One family may be enough; a pair may help when roles differ.
- Use colour roles for information and emphasis. A token system should make consistent editing possible, without prescribing six variables, a dark theme, or one accent.
- Use spacing, density, alignment, media crop, and transitions between sections to create rhythm. Asymmetry, overlap, borders, numbering, and editorial labels need a reason in the content.
- Materials such as grain, blur, shadows, scrims, or depth are optional. Add only what supports the subject, improves legibility, or communicates a real interaction.
- Keep significant content in semantic HTML. An artwork layer may support the page, but cannot replace readable copy, controls, navigation, or accessible states.

## Preserve the direction across devices

Name the visual anchors that must survive implementation: title shape and line breaks, dominant media or deliberate lack of it, negative space, colour/material roles, hierarchy, and first action. Compare real renders at matching proportions. Mobile is a composition of its own, with viable crop, readable copy, visible action, and direct navigation.

A direction can be restrained or expressive. Judge whether each choice serves this visitor and brief. The skill cannot guarantee taste; evidence from the actual page makes mismatches visible.
